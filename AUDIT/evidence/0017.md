# Audit 0017 — bound HTTP header buffering before the decoder completes

## Finding: confirmed, and the source comment was wrong

The source said NIO "has no field-count or total-size limit, and `HTTPDecoder`
exposes none to configure". That was true for older NIO; the pinned
SwiftNIO **2.100.0** (`Package.resolved`, revision
`57c0a08a331aaea9f5d7a932ad94ef43be942a95`) exposes
`NIOHTTPDecoderLimitConfiguration` with `maxHeaderFieldSize`,
`maxHeaderListSize` and `maxHeaderFieldCount`, and
`configureHTTPServerPipeline(..., withDecoderLimitConfiguration:)` accepts it.
`HTTPServer.swift` called the overload without it, so the decoder ran with its
defaults (80 KiB per field, 2 MiB per list, 256 fields) and buffered a
never-terminated head up to 2 MiB per connection. The application's 16 KiB /
128-field check runs only after the head is emitted, so it could not bound that
buffering at all.

Reproduced with two raw-socket tests that never write the terminating CRLF:

    swift test --no-parallel --filter HTTPServerTests
    -> Test run with 39 tests in 1 suite failed after 17.080 seconds with 3 issues.

- `incrementallySuppliedOversizedHeadersAreRefusedBeforeTheHeadCompletes`:
  40 KiB of small fields, no final CRLF -> no response, `.timeout` after 2 s.
- `aSingleOversizedHeaderFieldIsRefusedBeforeTheHeadCompletes`: a 20 KiB field
  value, no CRLF -> no response, `.timeout`.
- The third issue was the new boundary test's own off-by-one (it counted
  `Connection` as a field), fixed in the test.

## Change

`sources/TinyTitanServer/Core/HTTPServer.swift`:

- `maximumDecoderHeaderListBytes = 32 KiB`, `decoderLimitConfiguration` with
  `maxHeaderFieldSize = 16 KiB` (the application's whole byte budget: no field
  that large was ever acceptable), `maxHeaderListSize = 32 KiB`, and
  `maxHeaderFieldCount = 256`.
- The listener passes it to `configureHTTPServerPipeline`. A head over the
  bound gets NIO's `HTTPServerProtocolErrorHandler` 400 and the channel closes,
  *before* `HTTPRequestHead` exists.
- The field count stays at NIO's 256 rather than 128 so a completed 129-field
  head is still answered by the application's documented 431
  (`request_headers_too_large`); the decoder bound is the outer backstop for a
  head that never completes. The comment on `maximumRequestHeaderBytes` was
  corrected to say exactly this.

The application byte/field checks, the body cap, 413 draining, SSE,
keep-alive, pipelining, HEAD and 405/404 behaviour are unchanged.

## Tests

`tests/TinyTitanServer/HTTPServerTests.swift`:

- `incrementallySuppliedOversizedHeadersAreRefusedBeforeTheHeadCompletes` —
  never terminates the head, asserts 400 before it and zero generations.
- `aSingleOversizedHeaderFieldIsRefusedBeforeTheHeadCompletes` — a 20 KiB field
  value, no CRLF, 400, zero generations.
- `headerBlockOverTheApplicationLimitButUnderTheDecoderLimitIs431` — ~20 KiB
  completed head gets the application 431, not the decoder 400.
- `headersAtBothApplicationLimitsAreServed` — exactly 128 fields, and a ~15 KiB
  block, both 200.
- `decoderLimitsBoundAHeadBeforeItCompletes` — pins the installed values
  (32 KiB list, 16 KiB field, 256 fields); 32 KiB x 64 connections is ~2 MiB
  before any body.
- `chunkedRequestWithTrailerStillRoutes` and `bodyLargerThanTheHeaderBudgetStillRoutes`
  — framing/body paths unaffected.
- Existing `anOversizedHeaderBlockIsRefused` (431), HEAD, pipelining, SSE,
  keep-alive and stalled-body tests pass unchanged.

Passing after:

    swift test --no-parallel --filter HTTPServerTests
    -> Test run with 40 tests in 1 suite passed after 13.242 seconds.

Gates: `swift-format`, `swiftlint` 0.65.1 --strict, `file-length`,
`func-length` all ok.

## Residual risk

- A client can still make the decoder buffer up to 32 KiB per connection plus
  the 1 MiB body; that is the new bound, not zero. 64 concurrent connections
  cap it at roughly 2 MiB of headers.
- The idle deadline remains activity-based: a client sending one byte every
  <120 s keeps the connection open, but the head buffer cannot grow past the
  decoder bound.
- No dependency was upgraded; the limits are in the pinned 2.100.0.
